Who I Am

About Me

Student. Engineer-in-Training. Responsible Citizen.

I am Leonard Labarosa, a BS Computer Engineering student at Northwest Samar State University. There was a time when I thought understanding myself was simple — knowing my strengths, weaknesses, hobbies, and dreams. But as I continue my journey, I realized the "Self" is much deeper than I first understood.

This portfolio is my personal reflection across the six dimensions of the Self: physical, sexual, material/economic, spiritual, political, and digital. It is not only a school requirement — it is a reflection of my journey, my struggles, my growth, and the kind of person I aspire to become.

Technology is powerful, but without discipline, ethics, and self-awareness, it can also become harmful. I believe personal growth and technological growth must go together.

Personal Journey

Journey Reflection

A timeline of growth, challenges, and the lessons that shaped my identity — because understanding the self is a lifelong process built through experiences, failures, and continuous improvement.

The Beginning

Past Self — Who I Was

Growing up, I never paid much attention to physical health, financial discipline, or civic responsibility. I thought knowing my strengths, weaknesses, hobbies, and dreams was already enough to understand myself. Success felt simple — finish requirements, attend classes, and move forward.

The Struggle

Challenges Faced

College life brought academic pressure that led to irregular sleep, poor eating habits, and mental exhaustion. I experienced moments of being physically present but mentally drained — sacrificing rest for deadlines and neglecting well-being under the weight of responsibilities I was still learning to manage.

The Turning Point

Growth & Realizations

Creating this portfolio became a genuine turning point. Reflecting across six dimensions of the self revealed that personal growth is deeply connected: physical health affects productivity, spirituality guides decisions, digital behavior reflects character, and responsibility extends far beyond the classroom into community and society.

The Wisdom

Key Lessons Learned

Discipline is not about perfection — it is about continuously trying to improve. Self-care is a responsibility, not a reward. Financial wisdom reflects maturity. Intelligence alone is incomplete without integrity. And becoming successful starts with understanding and improving yourself first.

The Vision

Future Goals

To graduate as a Computer Engineer who combines technical knowledge with responsibility, empathy, and discipline. I aspire to use technology to help people — protecting security, privacy, and human dignity — while continuing to grow not only in skills and knowledge but also in character and values.
